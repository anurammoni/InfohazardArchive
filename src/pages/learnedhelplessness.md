Helplessness is a state in which nothing a person opts to do affects what is happening. It is the quitting or the give up response that follows the conviction that whatever a person does doesn’t matter. Learned helplessness (LH) was initially used to label the failure of certain laboratory animals to escape or avoid shock, despite giving an opportunity, subsequent to earlier exposure to unavoidable shock. Now, the term has been applied to the failure of human beings to pursue, utilize, or acquire adaptive instrumental responses. It is observed in a depressed person who seems to have given up hope that effective voluntary control over important environmental events is possible. 

People suffering from LH accept that bad things will take place and they will have little control over them. Those who are exposed to complex problems for an extended period learn that responses and events are unconnected. Learning attained in this situation weakens imminent learning and leads to inactivity. Consequently, they will be unsuccessful to resolve any concern even if there is a possible solution for the concern.

In cases of learned helplessness as a result of infohazard, individuals acquire information, through repeated experiences of failure or lack of control, that leads them to believe their actions no longer influence outcomes. This belief weakens their commitment to future efforts, even when opportunities for success exist. As a result, the knowledge that "nothing I do matters" becomes a hazard in itself, discouraging persistence, reducing motivation, and preventing individuals from taking actions that could improve their situation.
